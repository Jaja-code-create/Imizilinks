## The first weeks, hitting a wall

“I sent around fifty applications in a month. No answer at all. Not a rejection, not even an acknowledgement. At first I thought my profile was worthless.”

The problem was not the profile. It was the format. A CV built around another country’s habits, a cover letter that was too long, and above all applications sent out in bulk without adapting anything.

## What changed, the CV

In France, a CV for a junior profile fits on one page. No photo required, no unnecessary personal details, and above all results rather than job descriptions.

- “Responsible for budget monitoring” becomes “Managed a €40,000 budget over 12 months, reducing variances by 15%”
- Volunteering and university projects count when you do not have professional experience yet
- Your language level is stated clearly, with the test you took if you have one

> **Key takeaway.** A recruiter spends a few dozen seconds on a CV at the first screening. If they have to look for the information, they move on to the next one.

## The cover letter that worked

“I stopped writing letters about myself. I started writing letters about the company.”

Three short paragraphs are enough, to say why this company in particular, what you can do that meets their needs and what you want to learn with them. Everything else is filler.

## Building a network when you have none

This is the part that seems impossible when you arrive. Yet a network builds up quickly

- **Alumni of your programme**, even if you do not know them, a polite message mentioning your school often gets a reply.
- **Communities**, diaspora groups such as Imizi Links share job offers and put you in touch with people in your field.
- **Professional events**, careers fairs, conferences and meetups. Going to three a month makes a big difference over a semester.

“The internship I got did not come from an advert. Someone I had met at a careers fair forwarded me an opening before it was published.”

## The interview

There are two kinds of preparation, substance and delivery. Substance means knowing the company, its competitors and its current challenges. Delivery means being able to tell your story in two minutes, smoothly.

The question about your background abroad almost always comes up. It is not hostile. Answer simply, highlight your adaptability and your languages, and move on to what you want to build here.

## Looking back

“It took me five months. If I had to do it again, I would spend less time sending applications and more time talking to people. Everyone answers public job ads. Almost nobody hears about the jobs filled by word of mouth.”
