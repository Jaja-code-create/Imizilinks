## Who can apply

You can apply for housing benefit if you reside in France on a stable basis, you have a valid residence permit (a validated long-stay visa acting as a residence permit counts) and you pay rent for a home that is your main residence.

It doesn't matter whether you're in a student residence, a private studio or a flatshare: what counts is that your name is on the lease and you actually pay the rent.

## The amount depends on three things

- **Your rent** — the higher it is, the higher the benefit can be, but with a ceiling.
- **Your resources** — scholarships, wages and declared income.
- **Your housing situation** — the city, the type of home and the number of occupants.

The CAF online simulator gives an estimate in a few minutes. Do it before you even sign your lease: it can guide your choice of home.

## The documents to prepare

Gather everything before you start the application, otherwise the file stays on hold:

- Your residence permit or validated visa
- Your signed lease, with your name on it
- A bank account details slip (RIB) in your name, on a French account
- Your translated birth certificate, sometimes requested
- A rent certificate filled in by your landlord or residence

> **The point that blocks most people:** the French RIB. Without a bank account in France, the CAF cannot pay the benefit. Open your account as soon as you arrive, before you even apply.

## How to apply

Everything is done on the CAF website. You create an account, fill in the housing benefit application form, then upload the supporting documents. You are given a claimant number: note it, you'll need it for all your dealings.

Benefit is never paid for the month you submit the application. It starts the following month. So don't put off your application by a few weeks "while you settle in" — that's money lost.

## After the application

Track your file from your personal account. If a document is missing, the CAF flags it there — not always by email. Get into the habit of checking once a week until the file is validated.

Once benefit is granted, you must report any change: moving house, change of situation, end of lease. Retroactive adjustments are a pain to deal with.

## If the application is refused

A refusal isn't always final. It often comes from a missing document or a wrongly entered situation. Read the reason carefully, correct it, and resubmit. You can also contact the CAF for your department to understand the blockage.
