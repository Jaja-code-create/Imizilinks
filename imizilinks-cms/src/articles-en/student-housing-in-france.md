## When should you start looking?

As soon as your admission is confirmed, ideally two to three months before you arrive. Demand is very high at the start of the academic year in September, especially in Paris, Lyon and Bordeaux. The earlier you start, the more choice you have and the less likely you are to sign something in a hurry.

## CROUS university residences

These are among the cheapest options for students. You apply online at [messervices.etudiant.gouv.fr](https://www.messervices.etudiant.gouv.fr/). Places are limited and students with a grant have priority, but it is still worth applying. Ask the international relations office of your university or school too, as some institutions set aside rooms for their international students.

## Private student residences

They cost more, but they are often easier to secure from abroad. The rooms are furnished, everything is done online and services are included (internet, laundry, reception). Before you book, check the application fees and what the rent actually includes.

## Flat shares and private landlords

Sharing a flat lets you split the costs and live in more central areas. For a short stay (an internship or an exchange semester), the bail mobilité is a good option. It lasts from 1 to 10 months and the landlord cannot ask for a security deposit.

## Finding a guarantor with the Visale guarantee

This is often the main obstacle, as most landlords ask for a guarantor who lives in France. The Visale guarantee, offered free of charge by Action Logement, can play this role for students, under certain conditions. Apply on [visale.fr](https://www.visale.fr/) before you start your search. You will receive a certificate to add to your rental applications, which reassures landlords. Check your eligibility directly on the website.

## Documents to prepare

Put together a complete application in digital format

- passport or identity document;
- visa or residence permit;
- certificate of enrolment or proof of registration;
- proof of income (grant letter, bank transfer statements, guarantor’s statement);
- your Visale certificate, if you have one.

A complete application, sent quickly, often makes all the difference.

## Housing scams to avoid

Students looking from abroad are a frequent target. Be careful if

- you are asked to pay before visiting the property or signing a lease;
- the rent is much lower than other prices in the area;
- the landlord says they live abroad and offers to send you the keys by post;
- you are asked to pay by cash transfer or through a money transfer service.

If you cannot visit in person, ask for a video tour or have someone you trust visit the property for you. Good to know, for an unfurnished rental the security deposit cannot exceed one month’s rent excluding charges, and two months for a furnished rental.

## Once you have found a place

- Carry out a detailed check-in inventory (état des lieux), with photos.
- Take out home insurance, which is compulsory for tenants.
- Keep your lease safe, as it will serve as proof of address for other steps, for example to [open a bank account](/en/blog/opening-a-bank-account-on-arrival/).
