## What Campus France actually does

Campus France centralises applications from international students to French institutions, then forwards your file to the consulate for your student visa application. In other words, it is both your application platform and the first step towards your visa.

Until your Campus France application is approved, you cannot apply for a long-stay student visa. That is why any delay at this stage has a knock-on effect on everything else.

## The timetable to plan for

The procedure is spread over several months. The exact dates change every year, so always check the official timetable for your country on the Campus France website, but the logic stays the same

- **Autumn**, registration opens on the platform. This is the time to create your account and start gathering your documents.
- **Winter**, you submit your complete application and choose your programmes. Do not leave it to the last week.
- **Late winter to early spring**, an interview with a Campus France adviser in your country.
- **Spring**, institutions reply and you accept a place.
- **Summer**, visa appointment at the consulate, then departure.

> **The classic trap** is waiting until you have every document before creating your account. Create it as soon as registration opens, so you can complete it step by step and see exactly what is missing.

## Documents to prepare

The list varies depending on your country and level of study, but you will always need

- Your valid passport
- Your diplomas and transcripts for recent years
- A certified translation of any document that is not in French
- Proof of your level of French (or English, depending on the programme)
- A cover letter for each programme you apply to
- An up-to-date CV

Certified translations take time and are expensive. Start them first, even before you finalise your choice of programmes.

## Choosing your programmes well

You can apply to several programmes. Do not aim only at the most popular institutions, and build a list that mixes ambitious choices, realistic choices and at least one safe option.

Look at the city too. Housing costs vary enormously from one region to another, and an equivalent programme in a mid-sized city will cost you far less than in Paris. To prepare for this step, read our [student housing guide](/en/blog/student-housing-in-france/).

## The interview

The interview with the Campus France adviser is not an exam. The adviser wants to understand your plans, that is, why this programme, why France and what you intend to do afterwards.

Prepare a clear answer to these three questions and stay consistent with what you wrote in your cover letters. Inconsistencies between the application and the interview are what holds back the most applications.

## Proving your financial resources

You will need to show that you have enough money to live in France during your studies. This proof is requested for the visa, but prepare it in advance, whether it is a bank statement, a sponsor’s commitment or a grant letter.

Start early, get your translations done first, build a balanced list of programmes and prepare your plans for the interview. The procedure rewards people who are organised, not people who rush.
