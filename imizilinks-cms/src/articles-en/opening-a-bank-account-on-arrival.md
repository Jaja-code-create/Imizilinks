## The documents usually required

- A valid identity document, usually your passport
- Your residence permit or visa
- Proof of address in France
- Proof of your status, such as a certificate of enrolment, an employment contract or a job offer letter

> **Watch out for the proof of address catch-22.** The bank asks for one, but many landlords ask for French bank details (a RIB). Common solutions include a proof of accommodation signed by the person hosting you, with a copy of their identity document and one of their bills, or a certificate from a university residence. For the housing search itself, read our [student housing guide](/en/blog/student-housing-in-france/).

## Which bank to choose

There are three types of option, each working differently

- **Traditional banks**, with a branch and a dedicated adviser, useful when your situation is complex. They are often stricter about documents and more expensive.
- **Online banks**, cheaper and quick to open, but everything is handled remotely and your documents must be flawless.
- **App-based banks**, which you can open in a few minutes from your phone. Handy as a stopgap, but make sure the account details you get are French, otherwise some public bodies will refuse them.

Many banks have offers for students or newcomers, with reduced fees. Ask for them explicitly, as they are not always offered unprompted.

## The account opening appointment

Book an appointment rather than turning up unannounced, so your application can be handled in one go. Bring the originals and copies of every document.

The adviser will offer you extra services. You do not have to accept any of them to open an account.

## What to check before you sign

- **Account fees**, charged monthly, they add up.
- **The type of card**, a card with systematic authorisation prevents overdrafts and the fees that come with them.
- **Overdraft charges**, often the most expensive item.
- **Insurance and savings products**, rarely useful at the start. You can turn them down and come back to them later.

## If a bank refuses to open an account

Anyone living in France has the right to a basic bank account. If you are refused several times, you can contact the Banque de France, which will appoint a bank that must open an account for you with the essential services. Keep the refusal letters, as you will need them for this step.
